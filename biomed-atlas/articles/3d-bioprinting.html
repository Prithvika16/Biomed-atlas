Exploring 3D Bioprinting

An introduction to bioinks, bioprinting methods and tissue engineering research.

At a glance

3D bioprinting is an emerging research area that uses additive manufacturing approaches to deposit cells and biomaterials in organised patterns.

The science behind it

A bioink may contain cells, hydrogels or other biomaterials. Depending on the technique, material can be deposited through extrusion, droplets or light-based processes. Researchers study printability, cell viability, structure and maturation.

Applications and importance

Potential research applications include tissue models, disease modelling, drug testing and investigation of tissue repair. Printing fully functional, transplant-ready complex organs remains a substantial scientific challenge.

A note on interpretation

3D bioprinting is an active research field. Experimental results should not be interpreted as proof that printed tissues are ready for routine clinical transplantation.
