How Does a Pulse Oximeter Work?

A beginner-friendly guide to light-based pulse oximetry, oxygen saturation estimates and measurement limitations.

At a glance

A pulse oximeter estimates peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO₂) and pulse rate using light transmitted through or reflected from tissue, commonly at a fingertip. It is a non-invasive monitoring tool, but its reading is an estimate rather than a direct measurement of oxygen in arterial blood.

How does it work?

Most pulse oximeters use red and infrared light. Oxygenated and deoxygenated haemoglobin absorb these wavelengths differently. A photodetector measures the changing light signal associated with the pulse. The device processes this signal to estimate SpO₂ and pulse rate.

Important considerations

Motion, poor circulation, skin temperature, nail products, ambient light and device quality can affect readings. Accuracy may vary across people and circumstances. A pulse oximeter should not be used alone to diagnose a condition or replace medical assessment.

Applications and further learning

Pulse oximetry is widely used in clinical monitoring and some home-care settings. Correct placement, device instructions and clinical context matter. If someone feels unwell, they should seek appropriate medical advice rather than relying only on a device number.
