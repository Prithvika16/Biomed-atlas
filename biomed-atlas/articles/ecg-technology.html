Understanding ECG Technology

Learn how an electrocardiogram records the electrical activity of the heart.

At a glance

An electrocardiogram (ECG or EKG) records electrical signals associated with cardiac activity over time. It is a non-invasive measurement commonly used in clinical assessment.

The science behind it

Electrodes placed on the skin detect small voltage differences. An ECG device amplifies and filters these signals, then displays them as a waveform. Different leads observe the heart's electrical activity from different directions.

Applications and importance

ECG systems are used in hospitals, clinics, ambulances and some wearable devices. Signal quality can be affected by movement, electrode contact and electrical interference.

A note on interpretation

Only trained healthcare professionals should interpret ECG findings in clinical context. This page explains the technology and does not interpret personal ECG readings.
