How Does an MRI Machine Work?

A beginner-friendly introduction to magnetic resonance imaging, magnetic fields and radiofrequency signals.

At a glance

MRI uses a strong magnetic field, radiofrequency pulses and signal processing to create detailed images of structures inside the body. Unlike X-ray and CT imaging, MRI does not use ionising radiation.

The science behind it

The hydrogen nuclei in body tissues respond to the magnetic environment. Radiofrequency energy temporarily changes their alignment. As the nuclei return towards equilibrium, they produce signals that are detected by receiver coils. A computer processes these signals to construct images.

Applications and importance

MRI is used in many clinical contexts, including evaluation of soft tissues, the brain, spinal structures and joints. The choice of imaging method depends on the clinical question and patient-specific factors.

A note on interpretation

MRI systems are complex medical devices. Their operation requires controlled environments, safety screening and trained professionals. This overview is educational and is not a substitute for clinical advice.
