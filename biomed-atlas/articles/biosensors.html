Introduction to Biosensors

Learn the basic components of a biosensor and how biological recognition is converted into a measurable signal.

At a glance

A biosensor is an analytical device that combines a biological recognition element with a transducer to detect a target substance or biological event. Biosensors are used in research, environmental monitoring, food testing and healthcare applications.

How does it work?

A typical biosensor contains a recognition element, a transducer and a signal-processing or display system. The recognition element may be an enzyme, antibody, nucleic acid or cell. When the target interacts with it, the transducer converts the interaction into an electrical, optical, thermal or other measurable signal.

Important considerations

Performance is described using characteristics such as sensitivity, selectivity, detection range, response time, repeatability and stability. Interfering substances, sample preparation and calibration can influence results.

Applications and further learning

Glucose monitoring is a familiar example of biosensing technology. Other biosensor applications are being explored for biomarkers, pathogens and point-of-care testing. A sensor result must be interpreted within its validated intended use.
