What Is Tissue Engineering?

An introduction to how cells, scaffolds and biological signals are studied to support tissue repair and regeneration.

At a glance

Tissue engineering is an interdisciplinary research area that combines engineering and life sciences to develop biological substitutes or strategies that may restore, maintain or improve tissue function. It draws on cell biology, materials science, mechanics and chemical engineering.

How does it work?

Three commonly discussed elements are cells, scaffolds and signalling molecules. Cells provide biological activity. Scaffolds can offer a temporary structure for attachment and growth. Biochemical or physical signals may influence cell behaviour and tissue development. Not every approach uses all three elements in the same way.

Important considerations

Researchers investigate scaffold architecture, porosity, degradation, mechanical properties, nutrient transport and compatibility with cells. Laboratory success does not automatically mean a construct is ready for clinical use; extensive testing and regulatory review may be required.

Applications and further learning

Applications under investigation include skin, cartilage, bone and other tissue models. Tissue engineering remains a complex field with scientific, manufacturing, ethical and clinical challenges.
