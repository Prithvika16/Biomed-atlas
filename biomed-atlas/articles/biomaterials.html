What Are Biomaterials?

Discover material properties, biocompatibility and examples used in medical applications.

At a glance

A biomaterial is a material designed to interact with a biological system for a medical purpose. Examples include selected metals, polymers, ceramics and naturally derived materials.

The science behind it

Engineers evaluate properties such as strength, flexibility, corrosion resistance, degradation behaviour and biocompatibility. The appropriate material depends on the intended application, the surrounding tissue and the duration of contact.

Applications and importance

Biomaterials may be used in implants, dental materials, wound dressings, tissue engineering scaffolds and drug-delivery systems. Testing and regulatory evaluation are important parts of development.

A note on interpretation

A material's suitability is application-specific. Laboratory performance alone does not establish clinical safety or effectiveness.
