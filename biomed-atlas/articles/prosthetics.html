Understanding Prosthetics

Explore how artificial limbs restore function and how biomedical engineering is advancing prosthetic technology.

At a glance

A prosthesis is an artificial device designed to replace a missing body part and help an individual perform everyday activities.

What are prosthetics?

Prosthetics are artificial replacements for missing limbs or body parts. They are designed to improve mobility, independence and quality of life. Modern prosthetic systems combine mechanical engineering, electronics, materials science and biomedical technology.

How do prosthetic limbs work?

A prosthetic limb is designed to fit the user's residual limb and support specific movements. Some advanced upper-limb prostheses use sensors that detect electrical signals from muscles. These signals are processed by a controller to operate motors that move the artificial hand or fingers.

Types of prosthetics

Prosthetic devices include upper-limb prostheses such as artificial hands and arms, and lower-limb prostheses such as artificial feet and legs. They may be passive, body-powered or externally powered using electronic and motorised systems.

Innovations in prosthetic technology

Recent developments include myoelectric control, lightweight materials, advanced sensors, improved socket designs and research into sensory feedback. These technologies aim to make prosthetic devices more comfortable, responsive and functional.

The role of biomedical engineers

Biomedical engineers contribute to prosthetic design by developing materials, mechanical structures, electronic control systems and human-device interfaces. They also evaluate safety, comfort, durability and usability.

A note on interpretation

Prosthetic technology continues to evolve. Device selection and fitting require individual assessment by qualified healthcare and rehabilitation professionals.
