Prosthetics: Design and Function

Explore the components, control systems and user-centred design principles behind modern prosthetic limbs.

At a glance

A prosthesis is an externally applied device designed to replace or support a missing body part. Prosthetic limb design combines biomechanics, materials science, mechanical engineering, electronics and an understanding of the user’s goals.

How does it work?

A lower-limb prosthesis may include a socket, suspension system, structural components and a foot. Upper-limb systems may include terminal devices, joints and control interfaces. Materials are selected by considering strength, weight, durability, comfort and the intended activity.

Important considerations

Some powered prostheses use sensors and actuators, while other systems are passive or body-powered. Myoelectric systems can use electrical activity from residual muscles as one input for control. The appropriate design depends on the individual and requires professional assessment and fitting.

Applications and further learning

User-centred design considers comfort, fit, function, maintenance, affordability and participation in daily life. Prosthetic technology is not one-size-fits-all; rehabilitation teams and prosthetists play an important role.
